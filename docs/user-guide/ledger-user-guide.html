00What LEDGER is for

Every project collects costs in Procore: T&M tickets from the field, direct costs (receipts, expenses, payroll), subcontracts and purchase orders, and the change orders on them. Some of those costs get billed to the client; some are already covered by the contract; some we choose not to bill. Without a single record of which is which, costs slip through unbilled — or get billed twice.

LEDGER is that record. It lists every billable cost on a project, lets you bill it to the client in a few clicks (it builds the Change Event, Change Order and invoice in Procore for you), and remembers what happened to every item, so the next person on the project sees exactly what's left.

Every record has one of four statuses

Unbilled

Still needs a decision. This is the list to work down.

Billed

On a client invoice or draft change order — by LEDGER, or marked Already Billed.

Budgeted

Covered by the contract or budget. Never billed separately.

Written off

Not billed to the client, with a reason (warranty, goodwill, PM decision…).

A record can be a whole T&M ticket, direct cost or commitment, or individual lines on one — so you can bill part of something and decide on the rest later.

Two ways in

Section 1 · Project level
The LEDGER sidebar — opens inside Procore on any project (or on your phone). This is where you bill, mark and review a project's costs.
Section 2 · Company level
The LEDGER dashboard — a web page with every project at once: margin, invoicing and how many records are still unbilled, by PM, region and stage.

Signing in and access

Both use your Einbau ID — the same login as PUNCH, TALLY, HANDOFF and the other Einbau apps. Access is set in HELM. Most people have one of four LEDGER levels:

LevelIn short
ViewerCan see everything, change nothing.
PM / AccountingCan bill, mark records and change Project Settings; can undo their own marks.
AdminAll of the above, plus undo anyone's marks, source-record drill-down and bulk reconciliation.

If LEDGER says you don't have access, ask Ben to grant it in HELM. If a button you expect isn't there, your level doesn't include it.

LEDGER prepares; a person submits. Invoices LEDGER creates are left as drafts in Procore for you to review and submit. Questions or problems: contact Ben Wright.

The project sidebar

1.1Opening LEDGER

  1. Open the project in Procore (the Prime Contract page is a good place — LEDGER will bill to that contract).
  2. Open the side panel and choose LEDGER.
  3. Sign in with your Einbau ID the first time. LEDGER keeps you signed in for about two weeks of use.

Away from Procore — on a phone or laptop — go to ledger-sidebar.pages.dev, search for a project by name or number, and pick it. On a phone, use Add to Home Screen and LEDGER opens like an app.

The ⧉ button pops LEDGER out into a full browser window when the sidebar feels cramped.

1.3Project Settings

Per-project defaults that pre-fill every invoice: labour rates (regular, overtime, double time, per diem), markup for direct costs and for commitments, how lines are grouped, the billing mode (T&M or fixed price — normally taken from the Procore project type), and a default Prime Contract. Leave a field blank to use the company default. You can still override any of them on a single invoice.

1.4Creating an invoice

  1. Pick items. Three tabs: T&M Tickets, Direct Costs and Commitments. Tick whole items, or expand one and tick individual lines. You can mix all three in one invoice.
  2. Configure. Check the Prime Contract, invoice number (LEDGER suggests the next one), billing date and billing period. Adjust rates, markups and how lines are grouped. Your Project Settings fill these in.
  3. Invoice as… choose Change Order on the Prime Contract (normal) or Standalone — a new Prime Contract just for these costs, for pass-through items like materials or travel.
  4. Preview. See exactly what the client will see. Edit line descriptions; pop the preview out if it's long.
  5. Confirm and watch the progress. When it's done you get a link to the draft invoice — open it in Procore and submit it as usual.

Good to know

  • Markup is added for direct costs and commitments; T&M bills at your labour rates. Both default from Project Settings.
  • Commitment change orders (approved only) appear right under their subcontract, or on their own once the subcontract is billed.
  • Hours already billed on a T&M ticket can't be billed again from a subcontract (and vice versa). LEDGER blocks it.
  • Labour on a subcontract gets a warning first — it's usually better billed from the T&M ticket or marked as budgeted.
  • Older projects (created before October 11, 2026) show a reminder to clean up first: mark what's already been billed, budgeted or written off before billing commitments.

1.5Marking records you won't bill through LEDGER

From any list you can mark ticked items instead of billing them. Each mark moves them out of Unbilled:

MarkUse it when
Already BilledIt was invoiced outside LEDGER (by hand in Procore, or before LEDGER). Add the invoice number. It counts as billed and can't be billed again.
Mark as BudgetedIt's covered by the contract or budget — the client won't see it as a separate charge.
Write OffWe're absorbing it. Pick a reason (warranty, service call, goodwill, PM decision, other) and add a note.

1.6Reviewing a project

Review Project opens with a summary table — T&M, direct costs and commitments against Unbilled, Billed, Written off and Budgeted — then a tab for each status. Use it to work down the Unbilled list, check what's been billed and on which invoice, and Undo a mark or a draft push if something was wrong.

Undo only changes LEDGER's record. If you undo an invoice, delete it (and its change order) in Procore first — LEDGER will then let you bill those items again. PMs can undo their own marks; admins can undo anyone's.
"Reconciled as Already Billed by…" banner? An admin closed out this project's old records in bulk from the dashboard. Anything new since then shows as Unbilled as normal. Those bulk records can only be reopened together, by an admin.

2.1Reading the headline

  • Margin to date vs budgeted — margin earned so far on what's been invoiced, next to the margin the job was budgeted at. Green when a project is at or above budget, amber when behind, red when negative.
  • LEDGER records — how many T&M tickets, direct costs and commitments are Unbilled, Billed, Written off and Budgeted. Unbilled is the number to drive down.
  • Totals — costs, invoicing, contract value, what's left to invoice, and what's left to pay out on subcontracts.

Every headline number follows your filters: pick a department and you're looking at that PM's projects only.

Budgeted % marked Q is the original quote — original contract against original budget, excluding pass-through contracts — held until the project's budget is changed in Procore. This stops change orders from making budgeted margin look better than it is. Once budget changes are made, it follows Procore's live budget.

2.2Finding projects

Search by name or number, and filter by stage (tick several at once), region, department (our PMs) and invoicing (not fully invoiced / fully invoiced). Click any column heading to sort. Cancelled, warranty, overhead and inactive projects are left out.

2.3Opening a project

  1. Click a row. LEDGER refreshes that project from Procore so you're looking at today's numbers.
  2. The detail shows direct costs, subcontractor invoices, committed costs and record counts.
  3. Open in LEDGER ↗ jumps straight into that project's sidebar to bill or review it; Open in Procore ↗ opens the project itself.
  4. Admins also get Show source records — every direct cost, subcontractor invoice and owner invoice behind the numbers, with a warning if costs are sitting on codes that aren't in the budget.

2.4Clearing old records in bulk (admins)

Older projects carry hundreds of records nobody will review line by line. Admins can tick projects (or filter and tick all), choose Mark reconciled…, and set everything still Unbilled on them to Already Billed, Budgeted or Written Off in one go. LEDGER works through them in the background and flags each project "Reconciled". Records that arrive later still show as Unbilled. An admin can Reopen project to put the whole batch back to Unbilled.

2.5How fresh are the numbers?

LEDGER refreshes every active project from Procore at least daily on its own, and immediately when you open one. It pauses its background work whenever someone is using LEDGER, so billing is never slowed down by it. The Updated column shows when each project was last refreshed.

ROADMAP

Where LEDGER is going

What's planned, roughly in order. Plans change as the team uses LEDGER — tell Ben what would help you most.

Now

Permissions by role

Each person's LEDGER access follows their job role, set once in HELM — viewers can look, PMs and accounting can bill, admins can tidy up.

Now

LEDGER on your phone

Already installable from the browser. Next: full details shown inside the app, and larger, easier-to-tap controls for invoicing from site.

Next

Budgeted margin from the estimate

Use the margin on the approved estimate as each job's budgeted margin, so the dashboard compares every job to what it was sold at.

Next

Projects that start set up

When a won bid is handed off in HANDOFF, the new project arrives in LEDGER with its labour rates and markups already set.

Next

Project overview

One page per project: billed versus cost by source, what's waiting and for how long — plus, for quoted jobs, which billing periods have been invoiced and where the gaps are.

Next

Instant updates

The dashboard updates the moment something changes in Procore, instead of on its daily round.

Later · AI-assisted

A LEDGER assistant that knows your projects

Built on the project information already gathered by SCOUT and HANDOFF — the PO, the quote, the handoff brief — and on LEDGER's own billing records.

  • Scope review — "Is this ticket in scope?" answered against the actual PO and quote, before you bill or write it off.
  • Agentic billing — ask in Teams: "bill this week's T&M on KPS". It prepares the invoice, shows you, and only submits once you confirm. Every action is logged.
  • Quoted-project billing — progress invoices on fixed-price jobs prepared automatically against the contract's schedule of values.
  • Answers on demand — "What's unbilled on Chris's jobs?" or "How is margin tracking on Costco?" without opening a report.
Also later

Travel and per diem rules — mileage, per diem and travel time billed the same way on every job.  Flag a cost for removal — mark a cost that doesn't belong on the project so accounting can move it.
